Building & Scale

The rebuilt house uses the full width and depth of two substantial plots. Almost half the interior lies across two joined subterranean floors extending under the terrace and near garden.

One new house

Nos. 30 and 32 began as the two halves of a matched 1860s semi-detached villa pair. Both interiors were stripped to their structural shells, joined and replanned around one new principal stair, one formal entrance and one coherent garden elevation.

The street still carries its Victorian origin. The interior, rear elevation, outer sides, roofscape, services and circulation resolve as a single contemporary residence rather than adjoining houses with connecting doors.

Operations

Albury works because guests, the household, musicians, deliveries and event infrastructure have distinct routes. Service appears where it is useful and disappears where it should.

01

Main stair

A new architectural route through all seven levels, including controlled fire-rated access to the social and wellness basements.

02

Passenger lift

Serves every level, with controlled vestibules at the Studio Albury and Alex's private floor plus a step-free front arrival.

03

Service stair

Links the mews, kitchens, both basements, music floor, guest floors and top floor without crossing principal rooms.

04

Goods lift

Carries catering, laundry, trunks and cased instruments from mews staging to every occupied floor.

Three arrival lines

Formal guests use Upper Phillimore. Vehicles, staff, deliveries and instrument cases use the primary Duchess of Bedford Walk mews and secure upper-basement garage. A second mews opening remains for garden maintenance, controlled service and emergency access.

Household Staff

Albury has 36 permanent employees across nine departments, supported by five scheduled specialists and trusted providers. The scale reflects a 50,000-square-foot, seven-level house with a professional studio, spa, fashion archive, serious guest accommodation and regular entertaining. Ages are at the August 2025 story opening. Ottilie owns the house and hospitality operation; she does not own Alex's diary, career or personal office.

A household working together

Ottilie and Kit coordinate the house. Rafael leads food production; Crispin leads front-of-house service. Housekeeping, garment care, engineering, security and the studio team keep their own specialist responsibilities.

Daily readiness is shared across staggered rotas, with additional event and studio support drawn in when needed.

Garden placement: the Barbara Hepworth remains on the west border in every view and event state.

No. 34 acquisition projection

The detached villa remains intact as the staff house, with its own compact private terrace and lawn enclosed by clipped hedges, an integrated dark metal estate fence and a proper gated entrance. It is no longer presented as a second principal residence.

The outer side garden becomes a separately fenced kitchen garden of productive beds, trained fruit, herbs, gravel paths and one restrained greenhouse. Behind the compact staff-house enclosure, almost the entire former No. 34 plot remains one exceptionally large uninterrupted event lawn. Garden character comes from deep perimeter borders and boundary trees rather than shrub beds consuming the usable ground.

A single large formal arch is cut directly through the long north–south boundary hedge between the properties. Its east–west gravel cross-path takes guests straight from Albury's lawn into the No. 34 event ground, where a restrained perimeter walk sits inside the planted outer borders. The arch is not an entrance in the staff-house enclosure or a cross-hedge farther down the garden.

The proposal adds space for larger garden parties and catering circulation. A possible integrated capacity of approximately 500 remains subject to the completed scheme and event planning; it is not a certified outdoor-only capacity.

Projection: ownership is current; the staff-house enclosure, kitchen garden, hedge arch and expanded entertaining garden describe the intended landscape works.

Event overlays

The ordinary garden can absorb about one hundred guests across the terrace, near and middle lawn while retaining the central route and far-garden circulation.

For a seated dinner, one ballasted 48-by-52-foot clear-span marquee centres one hundred places on the lawn. It retains ten-foot side clearances, seven feet from the terrace and six feet before the far garden.

Albury’s current whole-house capacity remains approximately 300. The possible future integrated capacity of approximately 500 includes the completed Cedars scheme; it does not apply to the original lawn or the August opening condition.

Garden above, house below

The terrace and near garden sit above the engineered basement structure; the far planting retains deeper ground. The Cedars garden is ground rather than an extension of Albury’s basement roof. Its proposed staff enclosure, kitchen garden and event lawn remain a separate future scheme.
